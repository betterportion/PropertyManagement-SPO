# Maintenance requests, threads, projects and email

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md. Loads when you work with a file matching `paths`; the rest of the repo rules are in CLAUDE.md.

### Where a maintenance request is, and who fixed it

`CLOSED_MAINTENANCE_STATUSES` and `isClosedMaintenanceStatus` live in `shared/schema.ts`, beside the column they describe, because three places decide what "closed" means: the close-date stamping in `maintenanceStatus.ts`, the resident visibility window in `authz.ts`, and the range filter on the maintenance list. Three copies of that list would mean adding a fifth status silently widens or narrows what a household leader can read.

**A request has no delete in the app, by decision (#229, 2026-10-02): staff cancel it instead**, which matches "records are permanent" and keeps the history a vendor or house page reads. `DELETE /api/maintenance-requests/:id` remains for API use only (staff with `canManageMaintenance`, in the request's region); no screen calls it, so do not add a delete button.

**A staff request's region is its house's region.** The create and edit routes look the house up by `buildingAddress` (`getPropertyByAddress`) and take `region` from it, whatever the body says; an address that is not a house is a 400. A request tagged with one region but filed against a house in another is invisible to that house's RA while the household can still read it — the region and the house are two copies of one fact, so the server keeps them one. An older request whose address no longer matches a house keeps the region it has. The forms mirror it: picking a house fills in the region and locks it, on the request and contact dialogs alike.

**The location field suggests, it does not restrict.** `GET /api/maintenance-locations` returns the room names from that house's own walkthroughs, offered through a `<datalist>` so free text still works for anywhere the checklist has no word for. Free text alone will not group "living room" and "Living Rm", and grouping is the point — it is what lets somebody notice that these blinds have broken every year since SPO started renting this house. The staff create dialog uses the same vocabulary: the property picker writes `buildingAddress` and the room goes in `location`. It previously wrote the address into `location`, which meant that column held a room for a resident-filed request and an address for a staff-filed one — and no amount of grouping works across two meanings of one column. **A resident's house is taken from their account and the `propertyId` query parameter is ignored outright**: honouring it would turn this into a way to enumerate another house's rooms, which is a second read path into walkthrough data and the exact shape of both historic authorization gaps here.


**Contractor history is mostly a read over data that already existed.** `request_contacts` has linked vendors to requests all along and `invoices` already carry a `contactId`; there was nowhere to read it from. `/contacts/:id` collects the jobs, the invoices, the billing record and the notes. Two rules:

- **The linked requests are filtered by the *request's* region, not the contact's.** A vendor can work across regions, and reading their page must not become a way to see requests the caller could not otherwise open.
- **There is no rating field, and adding one would be a regression.** A star score on a vendor SPO may have to keep using invites arguments about the number and tells an incoming RA far less than a paragraph does. There is also no separate "project" entity: a project here always traces back to a request, and a second entity to keep in sync would decay.

### Request threads

Every request carries a thread — `maintenance_request_comments`, read on the request page at `/maintenance/:id` — so what the handyman said on the phone, who is coming when and what it cost stop evaporating out of text messages. Four rules, and where each lives:

- **Thread access is request access, with one tier gate on top.** `canReadComment` and `canPostComment` in `server/authz.ts` are `canReadMaintenanceRequest(...)` AND (staff OR the comment is shared). Every comment route decides through them and nothing else, so the house match (which a resident's own filing must pass too, #267), region scoping, the 120-day closed window and, for staff, the maintenance permission (`canViewMaintenance` or `canManageMaintenance`, checked inside `canReadMaintenanceRequest`) all reach a thread, its photos and its files with nothing implemented twice. Deleting a request photo additionally needs `canManageMaintenance` for staff. **Filtering is server-side**: the list route drops internal comments for a resident before responding. A client-side filter over a full fetch would ship "he quoted $4,200" to the browser of somebody who must not have it.
- **Internal by default.** `isInternal` defaults to true in the schema and the composer opens on Internal every time — the mistake this prevents is a cost pasted into an ordinary repair's thread with Shared left on from last time. The composer's visibility control is two buttons with `aria-pressed`, never a checkbox, because the current setting has to be readable at a glance. Visibility is fixed at posting: no route changes it, and no route edits a body. Delete is the author or an admin (`canDeleteComment`), after the read rule — a resident cannot act on an internal comment's id even if the author column were theirs.
- **A relayed comment says so.** `relaySource` ("Dave (handyman)") is what renders, as "Sarah, relaying Dave (handyman)"; `relayContactId` is the optional link to the contractor's record, which must exist and be in a region the poster can reach. Costs nothing now and keeps the history honest two years later.
- **No audit event.** A comment is neither access, money nor a document, and logging every one would bury the events the log exists for — the same reasoning that keeps photo downloads out. `routeAccess.test.ts` asserts the audit write is not called on a post.

**One file per comment, and it inherits the comment's visibility.** A quote PDF or a photo of what the contractor found goes up through `POST /api/maintenance-requests/:id/attachments` — the request's own route rather than `/api/upload-doc`, because that one refuses residents outright and a household may attach a photo to the shared comment it may post. The permission is the whole post rule (`canPostComment` with shared visibility), run **before** the multipart parser like every upload route, with the document limits, the same magic-byte check and the same upload layer, so `document.uploaded` fires without the route adding an event. The route returns `{ url, name }`; the client holds the pair until Post and sends it as `attachmentUrl` + `attachmentName`. The create route accepts the URL only in the `/uploads/<key>` shape (`UPLOAD_URL_PATTERN` in `shared/schema.ts`) **and only when the `uploads` row says this caller stored it** — a file inherits the visibility of every record that points at it, so a body naming a vendor's W-9 would otherwise hand that W-9 to the household. Reading it back goes through `findUploadReferences` as a `maintenanceRequestComment` reference, authorized by `canReadComment`: a file on an internal comment is never served to a resident, even one who can open the request. A second file is a second comment. **Deleting a comment removes the row and its file** through `removeDeletedRecordFiles`, unless another record still points at the file; the confirmation says the file goes too (JR's sign-off, 2026-09-28).

**A household posts shared, and only shared.** A resident account posts on the requests it may read — its own house's, whoever filed it (a request it filed itself elsewhere is not its to read back, #267) — and `canPostComment` is the read rule plus "shared only" with nothing more, so the 120-day closed window and the repairs-only type rule reach posting with nothing implemented twice: a leader cannot post on another house, on a request closed more than 120 days ago, or on a project. The create route **forces `isInternal: false` for a resident whatever the body says** — the household composer has no visibility control, so a body marked internal from that tier is a client mistake, not a grant, and it is stored shared rather than refused, the way the resident create route forces the type. Delete stays author-or-admin after the read rule, so a leader takes down their own comment and nobody else's. This is a resident write path, which is the shape of both historic authorization gaps here, and `routeAccess.test.ts` pairs every refusal with the write never reaching storage. The author — id, email and name — comes from the session and never from the body. Every posted comment is emailed to the people who can see it; that rule is under "Outbound email" below, and a household's comment flows through it unchanged: the housemate and the covering staff hear about it, the author does not.

### Request types

Every request is one of three types — `request`, `project`, `capex` — shown as **Repair / Project / Capital project** (`REQUEST_TYPE` in `client/src/lib/requestLabels.ts` is the one label map; `MAINTENANCE_REQUEST_TYPES` in `shared/schema.ts` is the vocabulary). ADR-0001 (`docs/adr/0001-projects-are-a-request-type.md`) records why projects and capital projects are a type on this table rather than a table of their own: they get status, rooms, contractor links and threads for free, and a house gets one list instead of three. **Wishlist stays a priority, never a type** — a wishlist capital project is a coherent thing.

The cost of that decision is the whole reason the type rule exists: bid amounts and contract terms now sit in a table a household leader can already read. So **a resident account reads type `request` and nothing else**, and the two halves — the column and the rule — shipped in one change and must never be split:

- **The rule is the first line of the resident branch of `canReadMaintenanceRequest`**, before the own-submission check and before the house match, because either of those is exactly what would otherwise let a project on the resident's own house through. It is derived from the type column and nothing else — never from status, priority or the request's text — and anything that is not the literal `request` is refused, so a missing type fails closed rather than reading as a repair. Every real call site passes a storage row, which always carries the column. Because every resident read path (the list, the detail route, contacts, photos, the upload route and the thread) goes through that one function, none of them needs a second implementation and none of them can drift. **The type filter is a staff default, and the resident rule is not a filter.** The list route reads no query string at all — the type and range filters live on the client — so `?type=project` from a household leader changes nothing; `routeAccess.test.ts` asserts a leader asking for a project or a capital project by name still gets only their repairs, with a staff control proving the parameter is ignored for everybody. If a server-side type filter is ever added, the resident rule must stay a separate condition applied before it.
- **The resident create route forces `type: "request"`** whatever the body says, the same way it forces the region, the house, the submitter and `status: "pending"` (a household reports a problem; filing one already closed would keep it off every open-work view). Residents cannot PATCH a request at all (the route is `requireStaff`), which is how "a resident can never set the type on an update" holds — it is asserted as the existing refusal in `routeAccess.test.ts`, with the write never reaching storage, rather than as a branch that would only exist to be tested.
- **Staff with `canManageMaintenance` set the type** in the create and edit dialogs, and the copy beside the control says plainly that projects and capital projects are not shown to residents, because changing a type away from Repair takes the request off the household's screen and somebody doing that should know before they save. Every request card and the request page carry a type badge with its word; the maintenance list has a type filter beside the others, defaulting to all types.

**Open work on the property page** is the four groups — Repairs, Projects, Capital projects, Wishlist, in that fixed order — of everything not yet closed on one house. `groupOpenWork` in `client/src/lib/maintenanceFilters.ts` is the partition: the wishlist is pulled out **first** (anything at wishlist priority, whatever its type), and the rest fall into their type group, so every open item lands in exactly one group and the four counts add up to the open total. It is computed on the client over the request list the property page already fetches — no route and no server rollup — and `client/src/components/PropertyOpenWork.tsx` renders it above the full request table, keeping an empty group on screen with a line saying so. Staff page only; a resident's repairs stay where they already were.

**The property page's history table carries the same type and range filters as the Maintenance page**, both defaulting to everything (all types, all closed requests), because a house's history is where a completed capital project belongs beside its closed repairs; `matchesType` and `closedWithinRange` from `maintenanceFilters.ts` are the rules, so the two screens cannot disagree about what a range or a type means. The filters narrow the table only — the open-work groups above it are never filtered, because the range is about history — and a table emptied by a filter says "Nothing matches these filters" rather than claiming nothing was ever reported. Client-side view filters like the Maintenance page's: the list route reads no query string and the resident type rule stays the separate server-side condition it is.

The same grouping appears at two more scopes. The **Maintenance page** has an "Open work" tab (`?view=open`, in the URL like the other filters) rendering the same component over whatever the filters above it select — one house once a house is picked, which is the primary view: an RA works one house at a time. The **dashboard** reads open work through `actionItems.ts` as one aggregated item per house — "Open work — Cleveland House · 2 repairs, 1 project" — keyed on the house's **address** (what the building filter reads, and a request outlives its property record), with no due date so it sits below every dated item, and linking to the Maintenance page filtered to that house. Never one item per request: that space belongs to maintenance triage, and the reasoning that kept the setup checklist to a single row only holds while this stays aggregated. The stat tiles carry the counts as two numbers, open repairs and open jobs, from `regionSummary.ts`. Staff with `canViewMaintenance` or `canManageMaintenance` only, region-scoped through `filterByRegion`, and failing closed on an empty `allowedRegions` — `routeAccess.test.ts` asserts an RA with no regions gets no open work at all.

### Projects and bids

A project or a capital project carries what a repair never does: the signed contract, what it was expected to cost and what it came to, when it is meant to happen, and the bids it drew. `isProjectType` and `formatTargetPeriod` sit in `shared/schema.ts` beside the type vocabulary, as `isClosedMaintenanceStatus` does, so the update route, the edit dialog and the project card cannot disagree about which types carry them.

- **The five project fields live on the request row and are null on a repair by construction.** The PATCH refuses any of them with a value on a `request`-type row (400, "Only projects and capital projects carry…"), and a project turned back into a repair has them cleared in the same write. A resident cannot set them because a resident cannot PATCH at all; the resident create route strips them with the type. **The contract is a link** to where the signed agreement lives (Drive or Adobe), scheme-checked by `httpUrlFromClient` like the property links, and never an upload — the copy that matters is the one with the signature on it. **Costs are amounts only**; the money moves in QuickBooks and Ramp. **A target period is a year, optionally a quarter, never a date**: a quarter without a year is refused, checked over the stored row as well as the patch, because an edit sends only the field it changes. Changing the contract link records `maintenance_request.documents_changed` — that it changed on which request, **never the link itself**. Costs are estimates, not money moving, and are not audited.
- **Bids are their own table, and every bid route is staff under the maintenance permission, region-checked through the request, and a 400 on a repair.** Reading needs the view flag; recording, editing, removing and accepting need manage. The contractor is a contact record (checked to exist and to be in the caller's region, the same rule as linking one to a request) or a typed `vendorName` — one of the two, checked on the merged row so an edit cannot clear the last one. A resident never reaches a bid because they never reach its parent, and the route tests assert it directly on a project on the resident's own house, because "never reaches" is the kind of claim a refactor quietly breaks.
- **At most one accepted bid, enforced on the server.** `POST /api/maintenance-request-bids/:id/accept` is the only writer of `accepted` (the insert schema omits it), and `acceptMaintenanceRequestBid(requestId, bidId)` un-accepts every other bid on the request in one transaction. The screen's confirmation says "Accepting this bid un-accepts the others."
- **The quote goes up through `POST /api/maintenance-requests/:id/bid-documents`**, a narrow route whose permission is exactly the bid write's — staff, manage, region, project — run before the multipart parser, with the document limits and the same magic-byte check and upload layer as `/api/upload-doc`, so `document.uploaded` fires without the route adding an event. The bid create and edit accept `documentUrl` only in the `/uploads/<key>` shape and only when the `uploads` row says this caller stored it (`ownUploadFromClient`, shared with comment attachments); an edit that leaves the document out of the body leaves it alone, so an RA editing a colleague's bid is not refused for a file they did not upload. Reading it back is the `maintenanceRequestBid` reference in `canReadUploadReference`: the request's region for staff, and **a resident is refused by name** rather than left to the request rule, because that rule would let a household leader through once the project was turned back into a repair, and the bids on it do not go anywhere when that happens.
- **Removing a bid is a hard delete and takes its quote with it** through `removeDeletedRecordFiles`, unless another record still points at the file. The confirmation names the file: "This removes the bid and deletes quote.pdf too."

On the request page these are two cards, `RequestProjectCard` and `RequestBids` (the quote goes through `CommentAttachmentField` pointed at the bid-document route), rendered for staff on a project or a capital project and for nobody else; the edit dialog shows the five fields only when the chosen type is not Repair. The first plan's 5.5 ("no separate project entity") stands for retrospective contractor history; prospective projects are the addendum's Phase 10 and ADR-0001.

### Rollups over maintenance history

`server/aggregates.ts` answers "what keeps happening?", where the Phase 5 filters answer "what happened?". Both are computed over the caller's **own visible requests**, so a rollup can never widen what somebody can see — a link to a request they cannot read contributes nothing, not even a count.

- **A recurring issue is one house, one room, one category, more than once.** The house is part of the key and always will be: "these blinds have broken every year" is a claim about *these* blinds. Room names are folded for case and whitespace as a backstop for what was typed before the location field started suggesting from the walkthrough vocabulary.
- **A callback is a repeat visit to the same problem**, which is a different claim from "did a lot of jobs" and the one that belongs in a conversation about whether to keep using somebody.
- **Only a reported repair can be a repeat** (#160). Scheduled upkeep (`submittedBy` is `SCHEDULED_REQUEST_SUBMITTER`, "Preventive schedule") and projects and capital projects have no grouping key, so they never make a recurring issue or a callback — a detector test and an extinguisher check both land as "Whole house / Safety Equipment", and a planned re-roof is a decision, not the roof failing again. They still count in a contractor's total and open jobs: they are work, only not a repeat.

### Outbound email

`server/notifications.ts` holds the message builders — pure, a record in and a message out — so the wording is testable without a mail provider and the content rule holds in one place: **names, dates, amounts and descriptions yes; a credential or a banking identifier never.** That is the audit log's rule, applied to email for the same reason: both leave the system and neither can be recalled.

Every builder returns `null` (or an empty list) when there is nothing to send, so a caller never has to tell "no message" from "a message that failed". Sends are fired with `void sendEmail(...)` rather than awaited: `sendEmail` already never throws, and eight round trips to a mail provider should not hold a response open for a courtesy attached to something that has already happened.

- **The acknowledgement is addressed off `submittedBy`, which is an email.** Reading it as a user id would send every acknowledgement to nowhere, silently.
- **A status email fires on the same condition as the audit event** — an actual status change — so an edit to a description emails nobody about nothing.
- **A household email goes to current residents only, one message per person.** Current is `isCurrentResident` in `shared/residents.ts`: active, and a stop date that has not passed (the stop day itself still counts), the same rule `isCurrentRosterMember` applies, because a roster row stays active after its stop date until somebody records the move-out (#259). A mail-out to people who moved out last spring is the kind of mistake that gets a tool abandoned, and one message per person means nobody's address is disclosed to the rest of the house. The recipients are listed on screen before sending. The audit summary records the house, the subject and the count — **never the body**, because a summary is bounded and a house mail-out can run to pages.
- **The lease renewal reminder** is a seasonal task keyed on the house and the renewal date, raised `LEASE_RENEWAL_NOTICE_DAYS` (60) ahead — the same horizon the lease action item uses, so the two cannot tell an RA different things about one date. It clears when `renewalDecision` moves off `undecided`, either way: the daily run (`closeStaleLeaseRenewalTasks`) marks the open task done, and does the same when the renewal date moves, whose new date has a new key (#263). It is filed as a **property** task, not safety like the other seasonal reminders: the region summary already counts the renewal off the house, and a safety category counted the one decision twice (#162). The "Renewals due" tile still counts a house marked `renewing` until its renewal date, matching the lease action item; only the task clears on the decision.

**Every new comment emails the people who can see it.** `commentEmail` in `notifications.ts` carries the comment, the author line with any relay ("Sarah Lee, relaying Dave (handyman)"), the request's title and house, an "Internal — staff only" marker when it is internal, and a link to `/maintenance/:id` only when `APP_URL` is set. Who gets it is **one pure function**, `commentRecipients` in `server/commentRecipients.ts` — the request, the comment and the candidate accounts in, `{ userId, email }` out — with four guards that are all required:

1. **Never the author.**
2. **Internal comments to staff only.** Not a second rule: every candidate is put through the real `canReadComment` via `authContextFor`, so the tier gate, the repairs-only type rule, the house match, region scoping and the 120-day closed window all reach email with nothing implemented twice. A resident is emailed exactly when the read rule says they could open the comment, and never about a project.
3. **Anyone with `commentEmailsEnabled === false` is dropped**, on either tier. The switch is on the user row; a person flips their own from the account menu (`PATCH /api/auth/me/notifications`) and an admin flips anybody's from the users table in Settings (`PATCH /api/users/:id/notifications`, `requireAdmin` like every account change). Neither is audited — a preference is not access, money or a document. The email-based re-link in `upsertUser` preserves it, so an admin's "off" survives the account's first sign-in.
4. **The function decides nothing about delivery.** Whether email is configured, and whether a send works, is `sendEmail`'s business.

On top of the read rule, staff are narrowed to **those who have already posted in the thread** plus **regional administrators whose regions cover the request and who hold `canViewMaintenance` or `canManageMaintenance`**; an **admin is emailed only for a thread they have posted in**, or every thread nationally would land in one inbox. A participant who has since lost the region or the maintenance permission is dropped — "the people who can see it" is the rule, not "the people who once did".

The route (`emailThreadAbout` in `routes.ts`) saves the comment first, then resolves the candidates in three queries — every account with its permissions row (`getAllUsersWithPermissions`, one join), the thread so far, and the properties for the house addresses — never one query per person, and fires one message per recipient through `notify`. A failure working out who to write to is logged and the comment still answers 201; a send that never settles does not hold the response. `APP_URL` is optional in `config.ts`: unset means no link, and only a value that is set but not http(s) fails the boot check, because everybody the email reaches clicks it.
