# Deposits

### Deposits

**SPO holds a deposit per resident and the portal is a ledger and a reminder — the money moves in QuickBooks and Ramp.** Amounts, dates, descriptions and references only; the financial-data rule applies here without exception.

**Visibility is admins and the finance team only.** Residents never see deposits, deductions, balances or statements, and a household leader or steward sees none of it either — every deduction route carries `requireStaff` on top of the finance flag, and there is a test asserting a leader holding both finance flags is still refused.

`shared/depositLedger.ts` owns the arithmetic, in **cents as integers**: splitting in floating-point dollars is how 33.333333333333336 ends up on a worksheet finance acts on. It is in `shared/` because the split must be shown and edited *before* it is saved, so the browser and the server have to compute it identically.

- **A split is stored as individual per-person line items, never a shared charge with a divisor.** This is the important part: a later edit must not silently re-divide somebody's already-settled balance. `splitGroupId` exists for provenance and display and **nothing ever recomputes from it**. The whole split is written in one `createDepositDeductions` call, so a house is never half-charged.
- **The remainder is spread a cent at a time from the top**, so nobody pays more than a cent above anybody else. $100 across 3 is 33.34/33.33/33.33; $250 across 7 is three of 35.72 then four of 35.71. Those worked examples are in the tests as hand-computed literals, never recomputed the way the code does.
- **A deposit never records more returned than held.** `returnedExceedsHeld` checks it in cents over the row as it will be (an edit sends only what changed), on create and edit alike.
- **A balance may go negative and says so.** Damage can exceed a deposit, and clamping to zero would hide the shortfall from the person who has to decide about it.
- **The legacy `deductionsNotes` is displayed as history and never parsed into rows.** It is free text written by people, and a migration that guessed would be wrong in ways nobody notices until a deposit is short.

**Return deadlines are admin-set days counted from the resident's move-out date** — when possession came back, not lease end; somebody can leave in April on a lease running to July. **A house's own `depositReturnDays` wins; otherwise its state's**, from `deposit_return_rules`, which an admin fills in at Settings → Deposit return deadlines (JR's decision, 2026-10-01, Phase 6). **Do not ship a state-to-deadline table in the code**: the per-state figures are entered by SPO once it has confirmed each state's rule, start empty, and are audited (`deposit_rule.changed`). The states SPO operates in have materially different rules (Arizona counts business days, Florida and Kansas are two-stage), and figures baked into the repo would be legal advice that goes stale silently. No setting means **no deadline** rather than a default standing in for one — an invented figure would be a legal determination the portal must not make — though the action-item feed still raises it, because a deposit held for somebody who has gone is worth surfacing either way. `DEPOSIT_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` raises it 30 days before a move-out so the money is ready rather than chased.

**The item escalates with age once somebody has gone**: past the deadline it reads "Deposit overdue — N days past the return deadline"; with no deadline for the house or its state, after `DEPOSIT_ESCALATE_AFTER_DAYS` (14) it reads "Deposit still held N days after move-out" and says no deadline is set. Mid-year departures used to fall through here.

**No setting means no deadline, but not no urgency.** Every house has `depositReturnDays` null the day this ships, and an undated action item sorts *below* every dated one — so a deposit with no deadline falls back to "due now" for a resident who has already left, which is exactly what it said before deadlines existed. Without that fallback, adding deadlines would have quietly pushed every held deposit to the bottom of the action-item feed. Only `held` and `statement_sent` are outstanding: `returned`, `withheld` and `partially_returned` all mean somebody has dealt with it, and leaving a withheld deposit up forever is a permanent false alarm.

**The statement is an internal worksheet for finance, not a document the portal issues.** There is deliberately no send path: **"Open in mail" builds a `mailto:` link** (`statementMailto` in `shared/depositStatement.ts`) that opens the RA's own mail client with the recipient, subject and body prefilled, and the RA sends from their own account — the portal never sends a financial document to a resident. A click is not proof of a send, so it records `statementProvidedOn` as today and leaves it editable. A body over `MAILTO_MAX_LENGTH` (1,800 characters, the conservative cross-client ceiling) goes to the clipboard and the mail opens addressed with the subject only; a house of eight with five lines each fits, and the test says so. The text itself is `statementText`, pure, with each split line saying "your share of $X across N people" (`shareOfSplit`, display only — nothing recomputes from a group). `statement_sent` is progress, not completion: the money is still held and the action-item feed keeps saying so until it goes back.

**The move-out damages worksheet** (`/walkthroughs/:id/damages`, `client/src/pages/DamagesWorksheet.tsx`) is how a move-out walkthrough becomes deductions (2026-09 RA review, item 7). Staff only; opens on a move-out that has been **submitted or reviewed** (a draft says "Mark it submitted first"). It lists every item recorded poor or damaged and not dismissed, each with an amount and who pays: **defaulting to the room's occupants** — residents whose `roomName` folds to the item's room — **and otherwise to the whole house on the walkthrough date** (`residentsActiveOn` in `shared/residents.ts`, the same rule the split dialog uses), with a per-person override and the `splitEvenly` shares shown live. A zero row stays listed as the record it was considered. **The screen writes nothing of its own:** each non-zero row goes through the single-deduction route (one person) or the split route (several), both now accepting `walkthroughItemId`, so the per-person rows, the cents arithmetic and the audit events are the ones that already exist; a row that already has deductions is shown as charged and not re-saved. Saves are per row, not all-or-nothing, and the toast says "Saved N of M" when one fails. Who can be charged is everybody living there on the walkthrough date **plus anybody already moved out whose deposit is still held** — a move-out walked after the move-outs were recorded is the normal case. The close-out list below marks a deposit **returned for the balance after deductions** (a shortfall keeps the box off; finance decides), takes the processor reference, and opens the statement. The "Deposit to return" action item now carries that same balance (`actionItems.ts` reads the deductions) — negative when overdrawn, in which case the row links to Finances instead of offering "Mark returned" — where it used to record the full amount held whatever had been deducted, a real bug fixed here. Recording `statementProvidedOn` on the mail click is by JR's decision in the 2026-09 review ("a click is not proof of a send", hence editable).

Every deduction added, changed or removed records an audit event naming the resident and the amount — one per person on a split, because one person's balance changing is what somebody may later have to account for. Routine two-year retention: this is money, not access history.
